# AFL RL vNext — progress 04: career persistence and correlated elite outcomes

## What was built

This stage addresses a major limitation in progress 03: future seasons were being combined as though they were independent.

That assumption is unrealistic. A player capable of a 100+ season is much more likely than an average player to produce another 100+ season in an adjacent year. Likewise, failure to establish tends to persist. Ignoring this dependence overstates the probability that a player will produce *at least one* strong season across five years.

A leakage-safe persistence audit and a one-factor Gaussian-copula prototype were added. The copula preserves the annual model's marginal probabilities while allowing outcomes across years to move together.

The held-out structure is:

- 2019–2020 origins used to select the persistence setting;
- the 2021 origin retained as an untouched test;
- all annual predictions were generated by models trained only on earlier origins.

## Confirmed career persistence

Observed annual elite events are strongly correlated across the five-year horizon:

| Threshold | Mean pairwise annual correlation | Adjacent-season correlation |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 90+ | 0.537 | 0.628 |
| 100+ | 0.498 | 0.606 |
| 110+ | 0.322 | 0.383 |

This confirms that the previous independence calculation was structurally wrong. A player's five annual elite probabilities cannot simply be treated as five unrelated chances.

## Untouched 2021-origin test

| Threshold | Actual at least one season | Independence prediction | Correlated prediction | Independence Brier | Correlated Brier |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 90+ | 7.88% | 11.02% | 8.85% | 0.04589 | **0.04197** |
| 100+ | 4.12% | 5.17% | 4.76% | 0.02689 | **0.02598** |
| 110+ | 2.17% | 1.94% | 2.25% | **0.01464** | 0.01467 |

The correlated model materially improves the 90+ and 100+ five-year probabilities. At 110+, the annual probabilities were already slightly conservative and the small sample does not support adding positive persistence to the probability-of-any calculation yet.

The selected latent persistence parameters were:

- 90+: 0.50;
- 100+: 0.40;
- 110+: 0.00 under the current validation objective.

The 110+ result should not be interpreted as elite seasons being independent—the observed event correlations are positive. It means that, given the present annual calibration and small number of 110+ outcomes, imposing additional dependence did not improve the held-out Brier score. This will be revisited with pooled cohorts and a hierarchical tail model.

## Why this matters for keeper values

Under independence, a player with several modest annual chances can receive an exaggerated five-year “at least one good season” probability. That can overvalue broad but shallow upside.

Correlated simulation distinguishes between:

- a player with a persistent high-quality career state, likely to produce multiple useful seasons;
- a player with five separate low-probability annual chances;
- a boom-or-bust player with a genuine elite tail;
- a player whose non-establishment risk persists across seasons.

This is necessary before career paths are passed through replacement and captaincy value. Keeper value depends not only on whether a player reaches 100 once, but whether strong production persists long enough to create multiple starting and captaincy seasons.

## Important limitation discovered

Generating the current-player five-year board by refitting all annual models remains too slow in the current prototype implementation. The present scripts repeatedly rebuild preprocessing and refit models for every lead, which exceeded the available execution window.

This is an engineering limitation rather than a modelling result. The next implementation should persist fitted model artifacts and calibration objects once, then load them for current-player inference. That will also make the pipeline reproducible and prevent training during production export.

## Current conclusion

The trajectory approach has cleared another conceptual hurdle. Annual outcomes are predictably persistent, and explicitly modelling that persistence improves five-year elite-event calibration—especially at the 90+ and 100+ levels that drive useful keeper production.

The next priority is no longer another probability model. It is a production-quality model-artifact pipeline and a coherent Monte Carlo career simulator that can be connected to the existing keeper-value layer.
